Between the first morning and polling day sit six jobs that run every day. Campaign GRID turns those six into one route the whole team can see.

The checkpoints

Six jobs a campaign does every day.

Each one is a working screen, not a slide. Field team leads see only their own zone.

01

Candidate schedule

SCHED

Events and appearances on one calendar, with the decisions waiting on the candidate or agent in a single queue.

02

Field deployment

FIELD

Teams placed by zone and polling district, with tasks, a live map, and a flag when work runs late.

03

Content approval

APPRV

Drafts go through a fact-check before they can be approved. Nothing is approved with the check open.

04

Media enquiries

MEDIA

Every journalist question logged with its deadline, so an urgent one is never buried in a chat thread.

05

Expenses against the cap

SEN

Spending recorded in exact sen against the statutory limit. It records the facts. It does not give compliance advice.

06

Incidents

TRIAGE

Permit problems, vehicle breakdowns and disruptions triaged by urgency, then closed with what was done.

The wall

A campaign and an office answer to different duties.

Political opinion is sensitive personal data. So Campaign GRID is built so that the two can never mix. Four clauses, built in — not written down as policy.

I.

No link to citizen records.

Campaign contacts have no connection to any office database. This is enforced by tests, not by a policy document.

II.

No voting intent, anywhere.

There is no field for it, not even an optional one, and no supporter estimates.

III.

Invisible to office deployments.

On an office system the campaign area does not exist at all. It is not hidden or locked — it is simply not there.

IV.

Facts, not legal advice.

Spending is recorded against the statutory cap. It is never blocked and never judged. Compliance stays with the election agent.
